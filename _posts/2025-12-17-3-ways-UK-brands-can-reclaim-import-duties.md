> Cross-border ecommerce returns can involve different UK customs routes. Returned Goods Relief and repayment of duty after export are not interchangeable: the relevant route depends on how the goods crossed the UK border, why they are being returned or exported, and what evidence is available.

For apparel, sporting-goods, and luxury brands, start by mapping the movement of returned stock before estimating a refund. A return rate or product category alone does not establish customs eligibility.

## First identify the direction of the goods

Ask whether the goods were **exported from the UK and are now being re-imported**, or were **imported into the UK and are now being sent abroad**. These are different customs events and may be governed by different rules.

### Goods exported from the UK and returned to the UK

UK Returned Goods Relief (RGR) may apply when goods previously exported from the UK are re-imported, subject to the applicable conditions. HMRC's guidance describes the usual three-year return period and requires the goods to be returned in the required state. The re-import must be declared correctly and evidence linking the returned goods to the original export may be needed.

Import VAT treatment has separate conditions. HMRC states that, for import VAT relief under RGR, the person who exported the goods must also be the person re-importing them. Do not assume that duty and VAT relief have identical requirements.

For an ecommerce retailer, the records to review can include the original export declaration, product or SKU identifiers, the return record, evidence of the goods' condition, and the re-import declaration. A customs specialist should confirm which evidence and conditions apply to the particular flow.

### Goods imported into the UK and later exported abroad

This is not, by itself, Returned Goods Relief: RGR concerns goods previously exported from the UK and then re-imported. A UK import followed by an export may need to be assessed under a different HMRC repayment, remission, or customs procedure, and the reason for export matters.

HMRC describes a separate route for some rejected imports, such as goods that are defective or do not meet contractual terms. Its current guidance says to apply at least 48 hours before packing the goods for re-export or destruction and gives a one-year claim limit for rejected-import claims. Other conditions apply to the goods and claimant. Ordinary customer change-of-mind returns should not be assumed to qualify. Check the [current HMRC conditions and deadlines](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/refunds-and-waivers-on-customs-debt) before describing a shipment as eligible.

## Build a returns evidence trail

For each market and return flow, gather:

- The import and export declarations associated with the goods.
- Product-level identifiers that can connect a returned item to the original shipment.
- Order, return, warehouse, and carrier records that show where and when the goods moved.
- The reason for the return and any records about the goods' condition or use.
- Evidence of who owned or handled the goods at each stage.
- Any repayment or relief claim already made for the same goods.

For multi-carrier or multi-warehouse operations, check whether these records can be joined reliably before designing a claim process. Keep UK customs duty and import VAT analysis separate, and avoid counting the same goods or duties twice.

## A practical review for cross-border retailers

1. Select one representative return flow and write down every border crossing.
2. Identify where the goods were originally imported and where they were exported.
3. Match sample returned SKUs to their customs declarations and logistics evidence.
4. Record why each item was returned and whether it was used, changed, repaired, or resold.
5. Ask a qualified customs specialist which UK procedure, if any, fits that flow and what deadlines apply.

The same review can be applied to apparel, sporting goods, and luxury goods, but the product category does not replace the transaction-specific eligibility test. High-value or frequent returns may make data quality especially important; they do not guarantee a refund.
