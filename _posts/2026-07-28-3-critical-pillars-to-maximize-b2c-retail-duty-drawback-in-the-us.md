## The 3 Pillars of a Successful Retail Duty Drawback Program

Duty drawback can recover **up to 99% of duties paid** on imported goods that are later exported or destroyed. For **B2C retail brands**, the process is uniquely complex due to the high volume of small-value shipments, frequent returns, and dispersed inventory. Based on our work with customers, the most successful programs are built on **three foundational pillars**: **Company Setup**, **Logistics**, and **Data & Documentation**. Let’s break them down.

---

### **Pillar 1: Company Setup – Laying the Legal and Operational Foundation**

Before filing a single claim, your business must be **properly registered and bonded** to participate in duty drawback programs. This pillar ensures you meet **U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)** requirements from day one.

#### **Key Actions:**
- **Register as an Importer of Record (IOR):**
  Your business must be the **official IOR** for the imported goods. This means your **EIN (Employer Identification Number)** must be on file with CBP, and you must be the party ultimately responsible for duty payments.

- **Secure an Import Customs Bond:**
  A **continuous customs bond** (typically 10% of the annual duties/taxes paid) is required to cover potential liabilities. For high-volume eCommerce importers, this bond acts as a financial guarantee to CBP that you’ll fulfill your obligations.

- **Register for the ACE Portal:**
  The **Automated Commercial Environment (ACE)** is CBP’s primary platform for trade processing.

### **Pillar 2: Logistics – Controlling Goods and Ownership**

CBP has **strict expectations** for how B2C retail drawback must be executed, particularly around **physical control** and **ownership** of goods. Unlike B2B scenarios, where shipments are often palletized and tracked in bulk, eCommerce returns are **frequent, small, and dispersed**, making compliance a logistical challenge.

#### **Key Actions:**
- **Maintain Ownership of Goods:**
  To qualify for drawback, you must **retain ownership** of the imported goods until they are either re-exported or destroyed
  *Example:* If a customer returns a product to your U.S. warehouse, you must **not** transfer ownership to a third-party liquidator before filing for drawback.

- **Track Goods with Precision:**
  Use **serial numbers, SKUs, or unique identifiers** to match returned items to their original import entries. CBP requires **granular proof** that the goods being claimed for drawback are the same ones that were imported.

- **Designate a Drawback-Specific Warehouse:**
  Some of our customers designate a **segregated area** in their fulfillment centers for goods earmarked for drawback.

- **Partner with a Drawback-Friendly 3PL:**
  If you outsource fulfillment, ensure your **3PL (Third-Party Logistics provider)** supports:
  - **Duty drawback tracking** in their WMS (Warehouse Management System).
  - **Automated reporting** of returns linked to original import entries.

### **Pillar 3: Data & Documentation – The Heart of Your Claim**

While the **data** (e.g., HTS codes, duty rates, entry numbers) forms the backbone of your claim, the **documentation** is what **substantiates** it in CBP’s eyes. For **rejected merchandise drawback** (the most common type for eCommerce), CBP scrutinizes proof that the goods were **imported, unsold, and re-exported or destroyed**.

#### **Key Actions:**
- **Automate Data Capture:**
  Ensure your **ERP, eCommerce platform, and customs broker systems** integrate to capture import, sales and return data.

- **Prepare the “Golden Triangle” of Documentation:**
  For every drawback claim, CBP expects proof of import, proof of export (or destruction) and proof of ownership.

## **Next Steps for Your Brand**

If you’re not yet leveraging duty drawback, start by **auditing your current process** against these three pillars. Ask yourself:
- Are we **registered as IOR** with a valid customs bond?
- Can we **prove ownership and control** of returned goods?
- Do we have **automated systems** to match imports to returns and generate CBP-compliant documentation?

For brands already filing claims, **review your rejection rates**. Are most denials due to **missing documentation** (Pillar 3) or **logistical gaps** (Pillar 2)? Addressing these can **dramatically improve approval rates**.
